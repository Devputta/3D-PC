# Human rules: copywriting

Words in an interface are design material. Each one should make something easier to understand or use.

## Voice

Plain, specific, a little dry. The voice of a good teacher at a workbench, not a brochure.

## Rules

1. **Say what it is.** "Turns the power supply's 12 volts into the roughly 1 volt a CPU wants" beats "Powers your CPU efficiently".
2. **No selling words.** Do not use: seamless, elevate, unlock, unleash, powerful, robust, cutting-edge, revolutionary, delve, dive in, game-changer, journey of discovery, supercharge.
3. **No filler openings.** Do not start with "In today's world", "Whether you're a beginner or an expert" or "Let's explore".
4. **Active voice.** "Click the part that stores your files" not "The part that stores files should be clicked".
5. **Name things how the reader would.** "Memory (RAM)" not "volatile main memory subsystem". Introduce the technical term once, in brackets, then use it.
6. **Buttons say what happens.** "Next question", "Reset view", "Try again". Not "Submit", "Continue", "Go".
7. **One action, one name.** If the button says "Isolate part", the state is "Isolate part", not "Focus mode" somewhere else.
8. **Sentence case.** Titles, buttons, labels.
9. **No exclamation marks.** Not in success messages, not in the quiz.
10. **No emoji.**
11. **No invented social proof.** No "Trusted by thousands", no fake quotes, no made-up counts.
12. **Do not claim what is not built.** If notes are saved only in the browser, say so in plain words.

## Errors and empty states

- Say what happened and what to do. "This browser can't draw the 3D board. WebGL is switched off or unavailable. Try another browser, or check that hardware acceleration is on."
- Do not apologise and do not joke.
- An empty state is an instruction. "Pick a part. Click anything on the board, or choose from the list on the left."

## Quiz feedback

- Right: "Correct. That's the CPU." and one sentence about it.
- Wrong: "Not quite. You picked the cooler. The answer is the CPU." then the one sentence.
- Skipped: "Skipped. It was the CPU."
- Never praise effort ("Great job!") and never mock a miss.

## Length

- Part summary: one sentence, under 90 characters if possible.
- Part body: two short paragraphs. First says what it does. Second says one thing most people get wrong or do not know.
- Journey step: two to three sentences.
- Quiz clue: one sentence, never contains the part's name.

## Numbers and units

- Write units with a space: 550 MB/s, 6 Gbit/s, 12 V.
- Give ranges, not false precision. "About 3 to 6 GHz".
- Use "typical" and "about" where a number varies. Never invent a precise figure.

## Test

Read the page out loud. If a sentence sounds like an advert, rewrite it. If it could be pasted onto any other product's page, rewrite it.
