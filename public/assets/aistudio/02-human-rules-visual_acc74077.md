# Human rules: visual

Generated sites look generated because they all reach for the same defaults. These rules replace the defaults with choices made for this subject. Treat each rule as something you can check by looking at the page.

## The principle

Every visual choice should be explainable by the subject. Boardwalk is about circuit boards and a workbench, so the look comes from a workbench: an anti-static mat, a multimeter, a cutting-mat grid. If a choice cannot be traced back to the subject, it is probably a default.

## Colour

1. One accent colour, used for one meaning. In Boardwalk the yellow means "this is the thing you are looking at": the selected callout, the active list row, the primary button. It is never decoration.
2. No purple-to-blue gradients. No gradient washes behind sections. No gradient text.
3. Avoid the other two common clusters: warm cream with a terracotta accent, and near-black with a neon green or orange accent.
4. Neutrals are tinted toward the palette (a grey-green mat), not pure grey and not pure black.
5. Dark mode is a real second design, not an inversion. Check contrast again in it.

## Type

6. Choose typefaces for the subject. Barlow comes from highway-sign lettering, so it reads like hardware labelling.
7. One family in two widths (Barlow and Barlow Condensed) is enough. Condensed for titles and numbers, regular for reading.
8. Sentence case everywhere. No all-caps labels with wide tracking above headings.
9. Do not put one word of a headline in italic or a different colour.
10. Reading text is 16 to 17 px with line height around 1.5, and lines no longer than about 60 characters.

## Structure

11. No card grids. No identical rounded boxes with identical soft shadows. Panels are flat areas separated by one-pixel rules.
12. Different things get different shapes. Controls have small radii (3 px), callouts are circles, panels have none. Do not apply one radius to everything.
13. Numbers are used only where they carry information. The callout numbers match the list numbers. The journey steps are a real sequence. Nothing else is numbered.
14. No eyebrow label above every heading. No middle-dot strings ("A · B · C"). No arrows tacked on to every button.
15. Borders and rules must mean something. A rule between rows of a table is information. A border around a section for style is noise.

## Layout

16. The main thing is big. The 3D stage takes the most room. Text panels stay narrow.
17. Left-align text. Centre-align almost nothing.
18. Do not add a hero, a feature trio, a logo wall or a call-to-action band. A tool opens on the tool.
19. The background has a purpose. The faint grid behind the board is a cutting mat and gives the eye a floor.

## Motion

20. One orchestrated moment: the board settling together on load.
21. Everything else that moves is a reply to a click (camera flying to a part, the explode slider).
22. No fade-and-slide-up on scroll. No hover animations on every element.
23. Respect reduced motion by skipping the camera tween and the opening settle.

## Imperfection that is allowed

Real designs have small inconsistencies that come from real decisions. A caption that changes when you hover is fine. The yellow trim stripe on the graphics card echoes the accent. Keep touches like these; do not smooth every one of them into uniformity.

## Final look

Take a screenshot. Cover the logo. Ask: could this be any other product? If yes, find the most generic part and change it.
